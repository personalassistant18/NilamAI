How Nilam works

  1. Read the surroundings. For the chosen point, Nilam asks OpenStreetMap (the free world map) for every road, shop, school, hospital, bank, bus stop, railway station, town centre and construction project nearby.
  2. Measure. It turns that into numbers: distance to the town centre, distance to the main road, whether the plot touches a road, how many shops are within 1 km, and so on.
  3. Score. Each factor gets points. Together they make the Location Score out of 100.
  4. Compare. With known prices, it finds the most similar plots (similar surroundings, nearby) and estimates the price from them, the way a valuer uses comparable sales.
  5. Learn. With 6 or more known prices, it also fits a model (ridge regression) that learns how much each factor moves prices in your area. It uses whichever method has the smaller error on the known prices.

Starting weights

How many points each factor can add to the Location Score. These are assumptions based on common real-estate practice. They are not learned from prices.

Limitations

  • Map gaps. OpenStreetMap is made by volunteers. In smaller towns many shops and services are not mapped yet, which lowers their score.
  • No built-in prices. Estimates come only from the known prices you add, and are only as good as those prices.
  • Things it can't see: legal status (patta, approvals), plot shape, flood risk, soil, water availability and future announcements.
  • Not a valuation report. It's a learning prototype, not a certified valuation.
